# ADR-007: Datastore for the payments ledger

## Context

The payments ledger records every charge, refund, carrier payout and adjustment for Keelwright shipments. It is currently a set of tables inside the monolith's MySQL 5.7 instance, which is end-of-life in October and has no point-in-time recovery configured. We need a new home for the ledger before the carrier payouts project starts in Q2.

Finance's month-end close runs a set of about 40 SQL reports that JOIN ledger_entries against invoices and customer_accounts directly on the primary database. Dmitri's team owns those queries and has asked that they keep working with minimal rewrites; the close has to finish inside two business days.

Current peak load is roughly 600 ledger writes per second during the Monday morning booking rush. The 18-month projection from the FP&A growth model tops out around 1,800 writes per second, which is comfortably within what a single well-tuned Postgres primary can sustain.

All payment processing runs in us-east-1, and there is no plan for a second region within the planning horizon.

## Decision Drivers

- Strong transactional guarantees: a payout and its ledger entries must commit atomically.
- Finance reporting must keep working without a separate pipeline.
- Small team: two backend engineers, no dedicated DBA.

## Considered Options

1. **PostgreSQL 14 on Amazon RDS (Multi-AZ)**
2. **DynamoDB** with single-table design
3. **Apache Cassandra** (self-managed)

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: **PostgreSQL 14 on RDS, Multi-AZ, in us-east-1.**

DynamoDB was rejected because the finance reports depend on ad-hoc multi-table JOINs, which would force us to build and operate an export pipeline just to keep month-end close working. Cassandra was rejected on operational cost: nobody on the team has run it, and its consistency model makes atomic payouts awkward.

## Consequences

- Good: ACID transactions, familiar SQL, finance queries port with minor changes.
- Good: RDS gives automated backups and point-in-time recovery.
- Bad: vertical scaling only; if write volume grows far beyond projections we will need to revisit partitioning or a different store.
- Bad: reporting queries share the primary with production writes; we will watch lock contention during close.
